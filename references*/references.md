# Verified Research Papers

This file provides the scholarly collection in a standalone form. The course requires at least 20 verified papers, meaningful categories, full bibliographic information, a scholarly link, and a one-line relevance explanation.

> **Final student action:** click each paper link before submission and confirm title, authors, year, venue, and link correspondence. This is required by the course and is not replaced by AI-assisted discovery.

## Survey and Review

1. **Pre-train, Prompt, and Predict: A Systematic Survey of Prompting Methods in Natural Language Processing** — Pengfei Liu, Weizhe Yuan, Jinlan Fu, Zhengbao Jiang, Hiroaki Hayashi, Graham Neubig (2023), *ACM Computing Surveys*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3560815) — Survey and taxonomy of prompt-based learning.
2. **A Systematic Survey of Prompt Engineering in Large Language Models: Techniques and Applications** — Pranab Sahoo, Ayush Kumar Singh, Sriparna Saha, Vinija Jain, Samrat Mondal, Aman Chadha (2024), *arXiv*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07927) — Organizes prompt-engineering techniques and applications.
3. **Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation** — Ziwei Ji et al. (2023), *ACM Computing Surveys*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3571730) — Background on generated-text reliability and verification.
4. **Holistic Evaluation of Language Models** — Rishi Bommasani, Percy Liang, Tony Lee, et al. (2023), *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15007) — Standardized and transparent LLM evaluation framework.

## Foundational and Prompting

5. **Language Models are Few-Shot Learners** — Tom B. Brown et al. (2020), *NeurIPS 2020*. [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/1457c0d6bfcb4967418bfb8ac142f64a-Abstract.html) — Foundational few-shot prompting study.
6. **Prompt Programming for Large Language Models: Beyond the Few-Shot Paradigm** — Laria Reynolds, Kyle McDonell (2021), *CHI Extended Abstracts*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411763.3451760) — Early framing of prompt programming.
7. **Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models** — Jason Wei et al. (2022), *NeurIPS 2022*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903) — Shows how reasoning prompts change model performance.
8. **Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners** — Takeshi Kojima et al. (2022), *NeurIPS 2022*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916) — Demonstrates prompt-only changes in reasoning behavior.
9. **Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models** — Xuezhi Wang et al. (2023), *ICLR 2023*. [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/self-consistency-improves-chain-of-thought-reasoning-in-language-models/) — Uses multiple sampled reasoning paths to improve consistency.
10. **Large Language Models Are Human-Level Prompt Engineers** — Yongchao Zhou et al. (2023), *ICLR 2023*. [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/zhou2023iclr-large/) — Automatic prompt generation and selection.
11. **Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-shot Performance of Language Models** — Zihao Zhao et al. (2021), *ICML 2021*. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zhao21c.html) — Studies instability from prompt format, examples, and ordering.
12. **Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations: What Makes In-Context Learning Work?** — Sewon Min et al. (2022), *EMNLP 2022*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.759/) — Analyzes what parts of demonstrations drive behavior.

## Prompt Sensitivity and Robustness

13. **Do Prompt-Based Models Really Understand the Meaning of Their Prompts?** — Albert Webson, Ellie Pavlick (2022), *NAACL 2022*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.167/) — Tests sensitivity to relevant, irrelevant, and misleading prompts.
14. **Fantastically Ordered Prompts and Where to Find Them: Overcoming Few-Shot Prompt Order Sensitivity** — Yao Lu et al. (2022), *ACL 2022*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.556/) — Measures few-shot example-order sensitivity.
15. **Measuring and Improving Consistency in Pretrained Language Models** — Yanai Elazar et al. (2021), *TACL*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.60/) — Introduces ParaRel and paraphrase consistency analysis.
16. **Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design or: How I Learned to Start Worrying About Prompt Formatting** — Melanie Sclar et al. (2024), *ICLR 2024*. [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2024/sclar2024iclr-quantifying/) — Direct evidence for formatting sensitivity.
17. **The Butterfly Effect of Altering Prompts: How Small Changes and Jailbreaks Affect Large Language Model Performance** — Abel Salinas, Fred Morstatter (2024), *Findings of ACL 2024*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.275/) — Finds large effects from tiny prompt perturbations.
18. **State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation** — Moran Mizrahi et al. (2024), *TACL*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.52/) — Motivates evaluation across multiple instruction paraphrases.

## Evaluation, Consistency, and Bias

19. **Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena** — Lianmin Zheng et al. (2023), *NeurIPS 2023*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) — Studies position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases in LLM judges.
20. **Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators** — Peiyi Wang et al. (2024), *ACL 2024*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/) — Demonstrates positional bias in LLM evaluation.
21. **What Did I Do Wrong? Quantifying LLMs' Sensitivity and Consistency to Prompt Engineering** — Federico Errica et al. (2025), *NAACL 2025*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.73/) — Defines sensitivity and consistency metrics for prompt rephrasings.
22. **The Effect of Sampling Temperature on Problem Solving in Large Language Models** — Matthew Renze (2024), *Findings of EMNLP 2024*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.432/) — Examines decoding temperature alongside prompt engineering.

## Recent Research

23. **Flaw or Artifact? Rethinking Prompt Sensitivity in Evaluating LLMs** — Andong Hua, Kenan Tang, Chenhe Gu, Jindong Gu, Eric Wong, Yao Qin (2025), *EMNLP 2025*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1006/) — Questions how much prompt sensitivity is caused by evaluation artifacts.
24. **Understanding the Prompt Sensitivity** — Yang Liu, Chenhui Chu (2026), *ACL 2026*. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2053/) — Recent theoretical analysis of prompt sensitivity mechanisms.
25. **Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting** — Mikhail Turpin, Julian Michael, Ethan Perez, Samuel R. Bowman (2023), *NeurIPS 2023*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388) — Shows why reasoning traces cannot automatically certify causal faithfulness.
26. **Non-determinism of 'Deterministic' LLM Settings** — B. Atil et al. (2024), *arXiv*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667) — Important control when separating prompt variance from other output variance.
